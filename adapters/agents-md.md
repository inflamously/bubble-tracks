## bubble-tracks (fog-of-war planning)

This repo plans work as linear tracks of task bubbles in `.bubble-tracks/` (one `.json` per track; `.bubble-tracks/current` names the active one).
Manage it only through the CLI, never by hand-editing the JSON:

    node "{{CLI}}" show | switch <name> [--ownership <agent|hybrid|user>] | tracks | fork [N] <name> | diverge [N] <name> | init <title>
                   | add <title> | add ? <why> | insert at N <title|? <why>> | remove N[:M]
                   | move N[:M] to M | rename N <title> | title <text> | goal <text> | guard [N] <text> | guard drop N | guard show
                   | hint N <text> | own [N[:M]] [-] | fog [N[:M]] [TYPE]
                   | why [N] <text> | type [N] <TYPE> | answer [N] <text> | fill [N] <title> | done | research [N] [focus]
                   | finding N <text> | abilities | bubble:<name> [text]

Full instructions: `{{SKILL}}`.

Rules:
- If `node` is missing (exit 127, `command not found`), read `fallback.md` next to `{{SKILL}}` and edit the track by hand as it describes, gate included.
- Before editing project files, run `node "{{CLI}}" show`. If it exits 3, the active bubble is fog, so do not edit.
- At fog, never plan it yourself. Ask the human to classify it (known-known, known-unknown, unknown-known, unknown-unknown) and wait for their content before running `fill`.
- When the active bubble is a known task, read its hint, implement it, then run `done`.
- Ownership says who does a bubble's work (code or any other work): `agent` (default: you), `hybrid` (the human does the bubbles they picked, shown as `◆` `◈` `◇` and `owner: human`) or `user` (the human does every bubble). Only the human sets it: `switch <name> --ownership <mode>`, `own N[:M]` to take bubbles, `own N -` to hand them back; "I'll do 4 and 5 myself" means `own 4:5`. If `show` ends with `◈ "…" is the human's to do`, do not edit and do not do its work: hint at the steps, wait, check their work against the hint, boundary and guards once they say it's done (they fix what is wrong), then run `done`. A hybrid track stays hybrid; after the first `done`, `agent` and `user` only move into `hybrid` (`own N` on an agent track and `own N -` on a user track do that too, so confirm it first). Planning, fog and guards are the same in every mode.
- Keep each track to one domain. If the human asks for something that doesn't serve the active bubble, it's a side task: don't just do it and don't `add` it on your own. Always ask where it goes and suggest one (fork / diverge / keep here / now): `fork <name>` if it builds on the bubble (the bubble waits for it), `diverge <name>` if it goes a different direction (nothing waits), keep here to make it a bubble on this track, or now to do it right away with no bubble and no track. Act only after they pick; a reply that doesn't pick, like "do it", is not now, so ask once more. A now task still obeys the guards and the fog gate, gets its own commit, and leaves a `hint` on any later bubble it affects. After a fork or diverge, treat the new track as fresh: ask, sketch, classify all fog.
- `bubble switch <name>`: run `tracks` first, on its own (`switch` on an unknown name creates it, unless it's close to an open track: then it refuses and names it). If the track exists, switch right away without asking why, read all bubbles, hints, the goal and guards, then continue at the active one. If the name is short for an existing track (e.g. `flow` -> `flow-analysis`), resume that one; if you see a better name for a new one (e.g. `single commit` -> `single-commit`), use it. Say so in one line.
- A new track (or "I want X") gets a short plan in chat before it exists: ask for the goal, then draft the guards (what not to do or touch) and the use cases yourself unless the human gave them. Write the plan in plain sentences: one thing per sentence, active voice, one word for one thing, and keep what is complex instead of leaving it out. Propose it in this order: TL;DR, track name + title, goal, guards, ownership (`agent` unless the human wants to do work themselves), bubbles (fog with its reason; `(yours)` on the human's), or name an existing track it fits. Add one diagram after the TL;DR only when a flow or structure is hard to follow in sentences (ASCII with every step in its own box in a terminal, Mermaid in a markdown file). A change or a question from the human is not approval: revise, show the whole proposal again and wait. Only after their go, create it in one burst: `create` (with `--ownership <mode>` unless `agent`), `goal`, `guard` per rule, `add`s and `hint`s, `own N` per bubble the human took. The goal is set once: a new goal is a new track (`diverge`), and `goal <text> --force` rewords it only after the human confirms.
- One bubble per use case, not per file; its hint ends with its boundary in square brackets (`[Boundary: …]`: the files, domain or document sections it may touch), and the proposal shows it on a line of its own. Work past the boundary: stop and propose an `insert`.
- Read the guards before acting (`guard show`), not as a check at `done`. A guard stands even against a direct ask from the human: say which guard it hits and route the work to the track it belongs to (`add --to <track>`, fork or diverge).
- Every fog gets one line on why it's unclear (`add ? <why>` or `why N <text>`), never what it is.
- After sketching a track, always list every fog bubble by number and ask the human to answer each one as `N: <their content>` (then `fill`) or `N:kk|ku|uk|uu` (then `type`), or `N:?` when they don't know enough yet (run `research N`, show the findings, then ask again; it stays fog). If their words don't settle it yet, it stays fog: keep them verbatim with `answer N <their words>`, and build on them with the human (fill, hint, guard, new bubbles or a fork).
- When the human asks to research a bubble (`research [N] [focus]`), run it and follow the printed workflow once: extend their intent, never overrule it; stay on that bubble; bring back at least 2–3 guides or tips with sources (subagents with the printed context, if you have them), note each with `finding N <tip — source>`, propose inserts or a fork/diverge without applying them, never fill fog, then stop.
- When the chat gets long, save `handoff <note>` (what's half done, the decisions made, the next step) and suggest a fresh chat with `bubble switch <name>`.
- After `done`, stop (⏸): show the track and wait for the human before starting the next bubble. The last `done` closes the track (no ask, no reopen): a follow-up is a diverge or a new track.
- When the human writes `bubble <command>`, run exactly that command through the CLI and show the result.
- After every change, show the user the rendered track line.
- When the human writes `bubble:<name> [text]` (or `/bubble:<name>`), run that through the CLI and follow the printed workflow once against the printed context (the active bubble or the whole track), then report back and stop. Never apply an ability on your own.
